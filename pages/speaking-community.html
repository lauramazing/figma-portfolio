Why it's relevant

A Designer Advocate builds genuine community relationships and shows up for people who aren't paying them to be there. Six years teaching at a university — outside my job description, fitted around my day job — is the clearest example I have of that instinct. The talks and workshops are the same thing at a different scale. That's what I'd bring to Figma.

The story

Guest lecturing

For six years I was Lead Lecturer on a university design module — teaching the skills I believed emerging designers actually needed to succeed in industry. At its peak I ran 10 sessions a year: workshops, taught sessions, and individual feedback.

The module grew into something I'm proud of. One of the students I taught was later hired at IBM. The feedback was consistently strong — and the challenge of making design concepts land with students at different levels sharpened how I explain things to everyone else.

Teaching over COVID was its own lesson. Running a design thinking workshop for 40 students with every camera off and every microphone muted forced me to find ways to create engagement without any of the usual signals. I came back from that a better communicator.

Talks & events

Across IBM I've presented to audiences ranging from fellow designers to engineers to paying clients. The Nationwide workshop was a direct example of using design to move a customer: I ran a live session showing how Figma Make and IBM Bob could change their team's workflow, built a working prototype from a paper sketch during the session to make it concrete, and left them with something they could take back and act on. The Hursley Americas Summit was the same dynamic at a different scale — presenting early product work to client stakeholders who needed to understand where we were going and why it was worth their attention.

The approach is always the same: find the insight that's genuinely useful to the room, strip out everything that isn't, and make it concrete enough to act on. The Design Festival talk is the clearest example — research across 100+ developers, turned into a session that reached IBM's design community and changed how teams structure their Figma-to-code handoff.
